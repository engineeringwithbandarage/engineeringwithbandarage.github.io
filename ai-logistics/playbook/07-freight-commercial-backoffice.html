Freight, Commercial and Back Office

Behind every truck and container is a pile of paperwork and messages: quote requests, load tenders, rate confirmations, bills of lading, customs entries, invoices, claims, "where is my shipment?" emails. Until recently this was the part of logistics AI could not touch, because it is unstructured. Large language models changed that, and it is now one of the fastest-moving areas.

Instant pricing: the 3-1-0 idea for freight

The book describes Ant Financial's MYbank "3-1-0" loan process: three minutes to apply, one second to approve, zero human involvement, with each application checked against thousands of risk rules. A freight quote is a similar decision: predict the cost to cover the load, add a margin that balances win probability against profit, respond instantly. Brokers and carriers that quote in seconds win freight that slower competitors never see.

Pricing is also where the book's experimentation and reinforcement-learning ideas apply directly: multi-armed bandit methods test price points while exploiting what already works, minimising "regret." Guardrails matter: floors, ceilings, and checks for customer-level fairness and contract terms.

Email-to-order: the C.H. Robinson case

C.H. Robinson, one of the largest freight brokers, published detailed results from applying generative AI across the shipment lifecycle in 2024:

The company stresses that the model alone is not the advantage: it works because it connects to the firm's own data on customers, lanes and carriers, can spot what is missing in a request, fill the gaps and then act in the operating systems. That is the AI factory (chapter 2) plus the platform interfaces (chapter 3).

Carrier sourcing and AI agents

Covering a load means calling or messaging carriers, negotiating, confirming and tracking. Recommendation models suggest which carriers are most likely to accept and perform; AI voice and chat agents now handle parts of the conversation. DHL, for example, has adopted AI agents from HappyRobot to handle routine calls and messages across operations. C.H. Robinson reports piloting generative AI to contact carriers when automated tracking updates fail.

Customs and trade documents

A single international shipment can involve dozens of documents. Document AI reads invoices, packing lists and bills of lading, extracts fields (item descriptions, quantities, values, container and vessel), proposes tariff (HS) classifications and pre-fills declarations. The industry is full of vendors in this space; their accuracy claims are rarely independently benchmarked.

Keep a human in the loop for classification

Customs classification errors carry duties, penalties and seizure risk, and small differences (raw versus processed material) change the code. Use AI to draft and to flag uncertain cases; keep a licensed broker accountable for the filing, and log every suggestion and override so the model can learn from corrections.

Freight audit, claims and finance

Invoice audit is a classic anomaly-detection problem: compare billed charges to contract rates and actual events (was there really a detention charge, did the shipment really need a liftgate?). Claims use photos and delivery evidence to assess damage and liability. Both have fast, measurable payback and a clean data loop, which makes them good early use cases.

Customer service

"Where is my shipment?" is the most common logistics customer question. An LLM assistant grounded in live shipment events and predicted ETAs can answer it accurately in any language, and hand off to a person with full context when it cannot. The grounding is essential: a fluent answer built on stale data does more damage than no answer.

Book lens: removing the human bottleneck, not the human

Freight back offices scale linearly with people: more shipments, more coordinators. Automating most routine messages breaks that link, exactly the "removing the human bottleneck from the critical path" pattern of Ant Financial and Amazon. The people remain for relationships, negotiation, complex exceptions and supervising the agents. Gartner also warns that over 40% of agentic AI projects may be cancelled by 2027 because of cost, unclear value or weak controls, so start with measured, well-bounded tasks.

Key takeaways

  • LLMs unlocked the unstructured side of logistics: emails, documents, calls and chats.
  • The value comes from connecting AI to your own data and systems so it can act, not from the model alone.
  • Instant pricing wins freight; test prices with guardrails.
  • Keep humans accountable for regulated decisions such as customs classification.
