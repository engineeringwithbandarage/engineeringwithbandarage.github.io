AI Use Cases for Logistics and Transportation: The Transformation Playbook

Most logistics companies already "use AI" somewhere: a routing engine, a forecasting tool, a chatbot. Few have changed how the company actually runs. This guide is about the difference. It catalogues where AI creates value in moving goods, then shows how to rebuild the operating model so those use cases compound instead of staying isolated pilots.

For: executives, operations leaders, data and AI teams No code required Road, rail, ocean, air, warehousing, 3PL and freight brokerage

Where the ideas come from

The backbone is Competing in the Age of AI: Strategy and Leadership When Algorithms and Networks Run the World by Marco Iansiti and Karim R. Lakhani (Harvard Business Review Press, 2020). The book argues that AI does not just improve individual tasks: it changes the operating model of the firm, the way it delivers value, and removes the limits on scale, scope and learning that have constrained companies for a century. Several of its central examples are logistics businesses in disguise: Amazon's fulfilment network, Ocado's automated warehouses and delivery routing, Uber's ride network.

The book gives the frameworks. This guide applies them to logistics and adds current evidence from operators, analysts and vendors (UPS, Amazon, DHL, Maersk, C.H. Robinson, Gartner, McKinsey and others), with sources listed at the end of each chapter.

How the guide is built

  • Foundations (chapters 1–3). Why AI changes the logistics firm, the "AI factory" that produces decisions at scale, and the operating architecture it needs. Read these first: every use case later depends on them.
  • Use cases (chapters 4–7). A catalogue organised by where the work happens: planning and visibility, the fleet, the warehouse, and the commercial and back-office functions. Each use case lists the decision it automates, the data it needs, the kind of algorithm and the value reported in practice.
  • Transformation (chapters 8–10). How to choose and sequence use cases, the staged roadmap from siloed data to an AI factory, and the governance and leadership that keep it safe.

Reading the numbers

Results quoted from companies and vendors are what they reported, not independent audits. Treat them as evidence of what is possible and of the order of magnitude, then build your own business case with your own data. Chapter 8 shows how.
