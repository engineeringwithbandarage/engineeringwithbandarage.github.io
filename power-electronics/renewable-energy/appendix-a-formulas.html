Formula Sheet

Every formula card from the tutorial on one page, in chapter order, with the notes that define its symbols. Each section links back to the chapter that derives it and works an example.

The running design in one line

8 × 375 W panels (MPP 300 V, 10 A) → boost, D = 0.25, 750 µH, 50 kHz → 400 V DC link, 1.2 mF → unipolar H-bridge, 20 kHz, m_a = 0.81 → LCL filter 680 µH / 4.7 µF / 170 µH → 230 V, 50 Hz grid.

Chapter 1: The Big Picture

Open chapter 1 →

Power and efficiency

P = V \cdot I, \qquad \eta = \frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}}

Efficiency η is the fraction of input power that reaches the output. Everything else becomes heat.

Linear regulator loss

P_{loss} = (V_{in} - V_{out}) \cdot I

The whole voltage difference is burned off as heat in the series device.

Ideal buck converter (Chapter 4)

V_{out} = D \cdot V_{in}, \qquad 0 \le D \le 1

Switch on for half of each cycle (D = 0.5) and a 400 V input gives a 200 V output, ideally with no loss at all.

Efficiency of a conversion chain

\eta_{total} = \eta_1 \cdot \eta_2 \cdot \ldots \cdot \eta_n

Example: boost at 98 %, inverter at 98 % and filter plus transformer at 99 % give 0.98 × 0.98 × 0.99 ≈ 95.1 % overall.

Chapter 2: Semiconductor Switches

Open chapter 2 →

Resistive: MOSFET, SiC, GaN

P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}

Use the RMS current through the switch, and R_{DS(on)} at the hot operating temperature: for silicon it can roughly double between 25 °C and 125 °C.

Threshold + resistance: IGBT, diode

P_{cond} = V_0 \cdot I_{avg} + r \cdot I_{rms}^2

V_0 is the knee voltage (V_{CE0} or V_{F0}) and r the slope resistance, both read off the datasheet's output characteristic.

Chapter 3: Switching, PWM & Losses

Open chapter 3 →

Period and duty cycle

T_s = \frac{1}{f_s}, \qquad D = \frac{t_{on}}{T_s}

At 50 kHz the period is 20 µs; a duty cycle of 0.3 means 6 µs on and 14 µs off.

How PWM is generated

\text{switch on while } v_{control} > v_{carrier}

A controller compares a control signal with a sawtooth or triangle carrier at f_s. In practice a microcontroller's PWM timer does this digitally.

Conduction

P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS(on)}

Or V_0 I_{avg} + r I_{rms}^2 for IGBTs and diodes (Chapter 2). Independent of f_s.

Switching (linear estimate)

P_{sw} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} \, V I \, (t_{on} + t_{off}) \, f_s

t_{on} and t_{off} are the total overlap times of each transition. A good first estimate before you have measured data.

Switching (from the datasheet)

P_{sw} = (E_{on} + E_{off}) \, f_s \, \frac{V}{V_{test}} \, \frac{I}{I_{test}}

Scale the datasheet energies from their test conditions to yours, using the same gate resistor and temperature where you can.

Output capacitance

P_{oss} = E_{oss} \, f_s \approx \tfrac{1}{2} \, C_{oss,eq} \, V^2 f_s

Energy stored in the device's own capacitance is dumped into the channel at every hard turn-on.

Diode reverse recovery

P_{rr} \approx Q_{rr} \, V f_s

Extra turn-on loss in the transistor, caused by the opposite diode's stored charge. Nearly zero with SiC Schottky diodes or GaN.

Gate drive

P_{g} = Q_g \, V_{drive} \, f_s

Dissipated mainly in the gate driver and gate resistor rather than the switch, but it sets the driver's power supply.

Average voltage error from dead time

\Delta V \approx t_d \, f_s \, V_{dc}

Its sign depends on the direction of the output current. In an inverter it distorts the sine wave near each zero crossing, which is why controllers compensate for it (Chapter 6).

Steady-state junction temperature

T_j = T_a + P_{loss} \left( R_{th(j\text{-}c)} + R_{th(c\text{-}h)} + R_{th(h\text{-}a)} \right)

Junction-to-case comes from the datasheet, case-to-heatsink from the interface material, and heatsink-to-ambient from the heatsink and its airflow.

Chapter 4: DC-DC Converters

Open chapter 4 →

Volt-second balance

\langle v_L \rangle = \frac{1}{T_s} \int_0^{T_s} v_L \, dt = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_{L,on} \cdot D + v_{L,off} \cdot (1 - D) = 0

The companion rule for capacitors, charge balance, says the capacitor current also averages to zero. It gives the current relationships.

Buck conversion ratio

(V_{in} - V_{out})\,D = V_{out}(1 - D) \;\Rightarrow\; V_{out} = D \, V_{in}

The output is always lower than the input. The average inductor current equals the load current.

Buck inductor ripple

\Delta I_L = \frac{V_{out}(1 - D)}{L \, f_s}

Peak-to-peak. Choose L to set this, typically 20–40 % of the load current.

Buck output voltage ripple

\Delta V_{out} = \frac{\Delta I_L}{8 \, C f_s}

Only the inductor's ripple current flows into the capacitor, which makes the buck output naturally smooth. Capacitor ESR adds \Delta I_L \cdot ESR.

Buck component stresses

V_S = V_D = V_{in}, \qquad I_{pk} = I_{out} + \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta I_L

Both the switch and the diode must block the full input voltage.

Boost conversion ratio

V_{in} D + (V_{in} - V_{out})(1 - D) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; V_{out} = \frac{V_{in}}{1 - D}

The output is always higher than the input. The average inductor current equals the input current, I_{in} = I_{out}/(1-D).

Boost inductor ripple

\Delta I_L = \frac{V_{in} \, D}{L \, f_s}

Because the inductor is in series with the input, the input current is smooth: good for a PV source.

Boost output voltage ripple

\Delta V_{out} \approx \frac{I_{out} \, D}{C \, f_s}

During the on-time the capacitor supplies the whole load current alone, so the boost output needs more capacitance than a buck's.

Boost component stresses

V_S = V_D = V_{out}, \qquad I_{D,avg} = I_{out}

The switch and diode block the full output voltage, and the diode carries the output current pulses.

Boost gain with inductor resistance

\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = \frac{1}{1 - D} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{r_L}{(1 - D)^2 R}}

R is the load resistance. The gain peaks where (1-D)^2 = r_L/R and then falls.

Buck-boost conversion ratio

V_{in} D + V_{out}(1 - D) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; V_{out} = -\frac{D}{1 - D} \, V_{in}

Below D = 0.5 it steps down, above it steps up. The switch must block V_{in} + |V_{out}|, the sum of both voltages, which is its main drawback.

CCM–DCM boundary

I_{L,avg} = \tfrac{1}{2} \, \Delta I_L

Below this average inductor current the converter is in DCM.

DCM gain (with K = 2L/(R T_s))

\text{Buck: } \frac{2}{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4K/D^2}} \qquad \text{Boost: } \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4D^2/K}}{2}

Valid only while the converter is actually in DCM. Controllers must cope with the change in behaviour across the boundary.

Chapter 5: Maximum Power Point Tracking

Open chapter 5 →

Single-diode PV model

I = I_{ph} - I_0 \left( e^{V / (n N_s V_T)} - 1 \right), \qquad I_{ph} \approx I_{sc} \cdot \frac{G}{1000\ \text{W/m}^2}

N_s cells in series, diode ideality factor n, thermal voltage V_T = kT/q \approx 25.7 mV at 25 °C, irradiance G. Full models add a series and a shunt resistance.

Duty cycle for a given PV voltage

D = 1 - \frac{V_{pv}}{V_{dc}}

300 V on a 400 V link: D = 0.25. On the hot roof, 252 V: D = 0.37. At half sun, 286 V: D = 0.29.

MPP condition

\frac{dP}{dV} = I + V \frac{dI}{dV} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{dI}{dV} = -\frac{I}{V}

Left of the MPP, dI/dV > -I/V: increase the voltage. Right of it, decrease. At it, stop.

ADC resolution

\text{LSB} = \frac{V_{FS}}{2^{N}}

A 12-bit converter scaled to 500 V full scale resolves 0.12 V. A 2 V MPPT step is about 16 LSB: well above quantisation, but noise and ripple must be filtered.

Shunt sizing

V_{sh} = I R_{sh}, \qquad P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh}

5 mΩ at 10 A gives 50 mV for the amplifier and dissipates 0.5 W. Hall-effect or fluxgate sensors avoid the loss and provide isolation.

Chapter 6: Inverters

Open chapter 6 →

Modulation index and output amplitude

m_a = \frac{\hat{V}_{ref}}{\hat{V}_{carrier}}, \qquad \hat{V}_1 = m_a V_{dc} \;\;(\text{H-bridge}), \qquad \hat{V}_1 = m_a \frac{V_{dc}}{2} \;\;(\text{half-bridge})

Linear for m_a \le 1. Above 1 (overmodulation) the output grows more slowly and low-order harmonics appear.

Maximum ripple, bipolar PWM

\Delta I_{max} = \frac{V_{dc}}{2 L f_s}

Two-level output, ripple at f_s.

Maximum ripple, unipolar PWM

\Delta I_{max} = \frac{V_{dc}}{8 L f_s}

Half the voltage step at twice the frequency: a quarter of the ripple.

Three-phase output with SPWM

V_{LL,rms} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}}\, m_a V_{dc} \approx 0.612\, m_a V_{dc}

Space-vector PWM or third-harmonic injection raise the linear limit by 2/\sqrt{3}, i.e. 15.5 %, to V_{LL,rms} = 0.707\,V_{dc}.

Space-vector dwell times (sector 1, 0 \le \theta \le 60^\circ)

T_1 = T_s \frac{\sqrt{3}\,|V_{ref}|}{V_{dc}} \sin(60^\circ - \theta), \quad T_2 = T_s \frac{\sqrt{3}\,|V_{ref}|}{V_{dc}} \sin\theta, \quad T_0 = T_s - T_1 - T_2

Splitting T_0 equally between 000 and 111 gives the symmetric pattern with the lowest ripple.

DC-link capacitance for single-phase power pulsation

C_{dc} = \frac{P}{2\pi f_{grid} \, V_{dc} \, \Delta V_{pp}}

From the energy swing P/\omega = C V_{dc} \Delta V_{pp}. The capacitor also carries a 100 Hz ripple current of P/(\sqrt{2}\,V_{dc}) rms, plus the switching-frequency current.

Chapter 7: Isolated DC-DC Converters

Open chapter 7 →

Flyback conversion ratio (CCM)

V_{out} = V_{in} \, \frac{N_s}{N_p} \, \frac{D}{1 - D}

The buck-boost ratio scaled by the turns ratio.

Flyback switch stress

V_{DS} = V_{in} + \frac{N_p}{N_s} V_{out} + V_{spike}

The reflected output adds to the input. The leakage spike is limited by an RCD clamp or an active clamp.

Forward conversion ratio

V_{out} = D \, V_{in} \, \frac{N_s}{N_p}, \qquad D \le \frac{1}{2} \;\; (\text{1:1 reset winding})

The reset needs as long as the on-time, so the duty cycle is limited to 0.5, and the switch blocks 2V_{in}.

Phase-shifted full bridge

V_{out} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} \, V_{in} \, D_{eff}

During the phase-shift transitions, the leakage inductance's energy discharges the switches' output capacitances, giving zero-voltage switching (ZVS) at medium and high load.

LLC gain (first-harmonic approximation)

M = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(1 + \lambda - \dfrac{\lambda}{f_n^2}\right)^2 + Q^2\left(f_n - \dfrac{1}{f_n}\right)^2}}

f_n = f_s/f_r with f_r = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{L_r C_r}); \lambda = L_r/L_m; Q = \sqrt{L_r/C_r}/R_{ac} with R_{ac} = 8 n^2 R_{load}/\pi^2. M = 1 corresponds to V_{out} = V_{in}/n for a full bridge.

DAB power transfer (single phase shift)

P = \frac{V_1 V_2' \, \varphi \, (\pi - |\varphi|)}{2\pi^2 f_s L}, \qquad P_{max} = \frac{V_1 V_2'}{8 f_s L} \;\text{at}\; \varphi = \pi/2

V_2' = n V_2 is the secondary voltage referred to the primary. The sign of \varphi sets the direction of power flow.

Chapter 8: Gate Drivers & Isolation

Open chapter 8 →

Peak gate current

I_{g,pk} = \frac{V_{on} - V_{off}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv}}

R_{g,int} is inside the device (datasheet); R_{drv} is the driver's output resistance.

Voltage transition time

t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,plateau}}

During the Miller plateau the gate voltage is stuck near V_{plateau}, so the current is (V_{on} - V_{plateau})/R_g.

Gate-drive power

P_{drv} = Q_g \, (V_{on} - V_{off}) \, f_s

Dissipated in the resistors and the driver, not in the switch.

Induced gate voltage

V_{GS,induced} \approx C_{gd} \, \frac{dv}{dt} \, (R_{g,off} + R_{g,int})

A first estimate that ignores C_{gs}, which in reality absorbs part of the current.

Bootstrap capacitor

C_{boot} \ge \frac{Q_g + I_{qbs} \, t_{on,max}}{\Delta V_{boot}}

I_{qbs}: the high-side driver's quiescent current; \Delta V_{boot}: the droop you accept before undervoltage lockout.

Chapter 9: Magnetics: Inductors & Transformers

Open chapter 9 →

Faraday: voltage sets flux change

v = N A_e \frac{dB}{dt}

The voltage across N turns on a core of effective area A_e. Volts × seconds determine the flux swing.

Flux density from current

B = \frac{L \, i}{N A_e}

Follows from N\Phi = Li. The peak current sets the peak flux density, which must stay below saturation.

Inductance of a gapped core

L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A_e}{l_g + l_e/\mu_r} \approx \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A_e}{l_g}

l_g: total air-gap length; l_e: magnetic path length; \mu_r: relative permeability of the core (thousands for ferrite).

Steinmetz core-loss equation

P_v = k \, f^{\alpha} \, \hat{B}^{\beta}

Loss per volume; \hat{B} is the peak AC flux amplitude. For ferrites, \alpha is typically 1.1–1.8 and \beta 2.3–3, from the datasheet's loss curves.

DC resistance

R_{dc} = \rho \frac{N \cdot MLT}{A_{cu}}, \qquad \rho_{Cu} = 1.72 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\text{m at 20 °C}

MLT: mean length of one turn. Copper's resistance rises by 0.39 % per kelvin: 31 % more at 100 °C.

Skin depth in copper

\delta \approx \frac{66}{\sqrt{f}} \ \text{mm} \quad (f \text{ in Hz})

0.30 mm at 50 kHz, 0.21 mm at 100 kHz. AC current crowds into this layer, so conductors much thicker than 2\delta waste copper at high frequency.

Area product for an inductor

A_p = W_a A_e \ge \frac{L \, I_{pk} \, I_{rms}}{B_{max} \, J \, K_u}

W_a: window area; J: current density (3–5 A/mm²); K_u: window fill factor (0.3–0.5 for round wire on a bobbin).

Primary turns for a square-wave drive

N_p = \frac{V}{4 f_s \hat{B} A_e}

From Faraday's law over a half-period: the flux swings from -\hat{B} to +\hat{B} in 1/(2f_s).

Chapter 10: Filters, EMI & PCB Layout

Open chapter 10 →

LCL resonant frequency

f_{res} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{L_1 + L_2}{L_1 L_2 C_f}}

Place it between about ten times the grid frequency and half the ripple frequency, away from both.

Ripple reaching the grid

\frac{i_g}{i_1} = \frac{1}{\left| 1 - \omega_{sw}^2 L_2 C_f \right|}

Evaluated at the ripple frequency, 2 f_s for unipolar PWM.

Base values for sizing C_f

Z_b = \frac{V_{grid}^2}{P}, \qquad C_b = \frac{1}{\omega_{grid} Z_b}

Keep C_f below about 5 % of C_b, so the reactive current it draws at 50 Hz stays small.

Passive damping resistor

R_d \approx \frac{1}{3\,\omega_{res} C_f}

In series with C_f. Active damping in the controller can replace it and save its loss.

Capacitive leakage current

i_{leak} = C_{pv} \, \frac{dv_{cm}}{dt}, \qquad v_{cm} = \frac{v_{AN} + v_{BN}}{2}

With bipolar PWM, v_{cm} stays at V_{dc}/2; with plain unipolar PWM it jumps by V_{dc}/2 at the switching frequency.

Second-order filter corner and attenuation

f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}, \qquad \text{attenuation} \approx 40\ \text{dB/decade above } f_c

A common-mode choke of 5 mH with two 4.7 nF Y capacitors gives f_c \approx 23 kHz and about 32 dB at 150 kHz.

Turn-off overshoot

\Delta V = L_{loop} \, \frac{di}{dt}

20 nH at 2 A/ns adds 40 V on top of the DC link.

Ringing frequency

f_{ring} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_{loop} C_{oss}}}

20 nH with 150 pF rings at about 92 MHz, right in the radiated-emission band.

Chapter 11: Control & Simulation

Open chapter 11 →

Averaged boost converter model

L \frac{d i_L}{dt} = v_{in} - d' \, v_{out}, \qquad C \frac{d v_{out}}{dt} = d' \, i_L - \frac{v_{out}}{R}

Setting the derivatives to zero gives back Chapter 4's results: V_{out} = V_{in}/D' and I_L = V_{out}/(D'R).

Boost control-to-output transfer function (voltage mode)

G_{vd}(s) = \frac{V_{out}}{D'} \cdot \frac{1 - s/\omega_z}{1 + \dfrac{s}{Q\omega_0} + \dfrac{s^2}{\omega_0^2}}, \qquad \omega_z = \frac{D'^2 R}{L}, \quad \omega_0 = \frac{D'}{\sqrt{LC}}, \quad Q = D' R \sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}

A double pole from L and C, and a zero in the right half-plane (RHP).

Inner current loop

G_{id}(s) \approx \frac{V_{out}}{sL}, \qquad K_p = \frac{2\pi f_c L}{V_{out}}, \qquad K_i = K_p \cdot 2\pi f_{z,PI}

A PI controller with crossover f_c and its zero f_{z,PI} about a decade lower.

Slope compensation

m_a \ge \frac{m_2}{2}, \qquad m_2 = \frac{V_{out} - V_{in}}{L} \;\;(\text{boost down-slope})

Enough compensation for every duty cycle the converter will meet.

Chapter 12: Thermal Design, Reliability & Protection

Open chapter 12 →

Junction temperature on a shared heatsink

T_{j,i} = T_a + R_{th(h\text{-}a)} \sum_k P_k + P_i \left( R_{th(j\text{-}c),i} + R_{th(c\text{-}h),i} \right)

Check the device with the highest P_i \times R_{th(j\text{-}h),i}: it runs hottest.

Coffin–Manson cycling lifetime (simplified)

N_f = A \, \Delta T_j^{-n}

Cycles to failure fall steeply with the swing \Delta T_j; n is typically around 4–6 for bond wires and solder. Manufacturers publish power-cycling curves that also depend on the mean temperature and the cycle duration.

Electrolytic lifetime (10-degree rule)

L = L_0 \cdot 2^{(T_0 - T_{hs})/10}

L_0: rated life at the rated temperature T_0; T_{hs}: the hot-spot temperature in use. Lifetime doubles for every 10 K cooler.

Hot-spot temperature from ripple current

T_{hs} = T_a + I_{rms}^2 \, ESR \, R_{th}

The 100 Hz ripple (5.3 A rms in our inverter) and the switching ripple both heat the capacitor from inside; share them across several parallel capacitors.
